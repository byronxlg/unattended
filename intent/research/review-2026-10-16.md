# Supervisor review of the day 1 landscape sweeps, 2026-10-16

Four read-only Codex jobs ran in parallel (ledger ids research-fleet-ops-d1,
research-prediction-markets-d1, research-agent-skills-d1, research-sdlc-telemetry-d1; 9 to 17
minutes each, rc 0, Codex weekly window at 4 percent after all four). Their outputs are copied
verbatim into this directory. This note is my reading of them against the selection criteria in
intent/README.md, and the shortlist I am carrying into candidate intents.

## Quality of the sweeps

All four did what the brief asked: primary sources, point-in-time counts marked "from page", "not
found" where a fact was not established, a candidate section with per-increment verification
commands. `gh api` was blocked by the sandbox in every job, so no last-push dates; release and
changelog dates stand in. Two corrections to the 2026-10-15 note came back: bashebr/ai-native-sdlc
does ship a detector (detect_bands.py), not config only; and Cronitor documents duration thresholds
and rolling-window assertions, so "job monitors have no duration analysis" is wrong.

Gaps I noted, none worth a re-run on their own (the candidate jobs below carry them):

- fleet-ops: tables are thin (two to four rows each); no row for Healthchecks self-hosted on a
  Mac, no cron or launchd linters beyond cronlint; section D never inspected `launchctl print`
  output formats, which is the riskiest part of a Fleet Audit build.
- prediction-markets: Polymarket terms page did not load, so storage rights are unverified. The
  material finding is that the Polymarket geoblock page lists New Zealand as close-only on the
  frontend and the API, and Kalshi's developer agreement forbids sharing API data. That cuts the
  family's usefulness to Byron, who trades from New Zealand.
- agent-skills: thorough. It found that Skillfold (Byron's own tool) already occupies the
  distribution niche, and that agentlint, driftlint, SkillGuard and NVIDIA SkillEvaluator sit
  close to any quality or drift product.
- sdlc-telemetry: thorough on schemas; it did not inspect this repo's own ledger or wrapper, which
  already produces the per-job receipt the pr-evidence candidate says is the missing link.

## Shortlist

Three go forward to candidate intents (day 2): pr-evidence, Fleet Audit, Skill Contract Check.

Reasons. Each is deterministic and offline-testable, so Codex can prove every increment with a
command, which is what the build phase needs. Each has a user in the room: the lab itself for
pr-evidence (bin/metrics must compute Test and Deploy indicators from exactly this join), Byron's
Mac mini for Fleet Audit (bin/tick is a launchd job), Byron's skills and Skillfold for Skill
Contract Check. The prediction-market family drops because the geoblock finding removes the
daily use and the data terms are hostile to a published tool. bands-run drops because four
existing implementations already overlap its core. Fleetbook and Repo Watch drop as weaker
versions of neighbours that stay.

What the candidate jobs must settle before day 3: for pr-evidence, whether this repo's ledger
plus a receipt hook can give exact session-to-commit joins (the riskiest assumption named in the
sweep); for Fleet Audit, what `launchctl print` and `launchctl list` actually emit on the host's
macOS version and whether a read-only job can observe it; for Skill Contract Check, whether the
contract adds anything beyond agentlint's existing checks for three of Byron's real skills.
