How the average works

Each distinct national poll ending in the last 30 calendar days gets equal weight. The window includes both its first and last day, using the displayed New Zealand date. Corrections replace the previous observation; the download keeps its history.

Every included poll must explicitly report the same named parties before we calculate seats. When only Others is blank, the Others remainder rule derives it as 100 minus the named shares for that row, only if the remainder is between 0 and 10 percentage points inclusive. Named parties are never derived. Affected pollsters: {{affected}}. Derived rows are labelled on the polls page and recorded in the derivations field alongside exclusions in the average JSON and CSV downloads. Missing is not zero. An incomplete window shows descriptive shares and coverage, with no seat calculation. Published totals are kept as reported, even when rounding means they do not add to 100%.

Shares use exact decimal-derived integers and a common scale. Quotients use exact fractions; only display percentages round, half up to one decimal place. Threshold watch includes parties between 2% and 7% in either the average or any poll in the window. The threshold line is 5%.

What the seats assume

2023 electorate seats assumed; 2026 has different boundaries. One unassigned electorate remains unprojected. List capacity not checked. Theoretical seats and the majority assume sufficient candidates; actual list members, vacancies, occupied House and definitive majority remain unknown. These are calculations, not a forecast of winners or proof a government will form. A tie at the allocation boundary needs a recorded lot result.

Reading these pages

Arial and Helvetica Neue echo familiar ballot-paper labels without downloading fonts. Names and counts carry the meaning alongside party colours. All information is readable without JavaScript. Data age is checked when the page is built; an open page does not update itself.
