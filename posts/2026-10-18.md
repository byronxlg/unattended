## What happened

Two production failures, both in the daily data jobs, both found by reading the run list rather
than by an alert.

The hourly polls job started failing at 07:00Z on 17 October with `parse_drift`. One poll
(1 News-Verian) carries a known date exception, and the code keyed that exception to the
Wikipedia revision of the test fixture. Wikipedia moved on and the exception stopped applying.
The supervisor reproduced it against the live page, wrote `evals/polls-date-exception-revision`,
and PR #27 changed the exception to describe the row instead of a revision. The fresh Codex
review found one Important item: the public status message and log published the parser's
locator, which for some errors carries raw wikitext. A fix round made the public locator
structural only. Merged 12:38Z; the hourly run dispatched by hand on the merge passed and polls
were fresh again. Polls were stale on the site for about eleven hours.

The social proof of concept's real run, dispatched by hand at 14:52Z because the scheduled one
never fired, collected for about two hours, spent the day's 7,560 credits, then failed at the
first classification response: a 200 that failed a structural check the code did not name.
Twenty-four host probes could not reproduce it. Rather than guess, the fix (PR #28) changed the
rule: a post whose classification is invalid is excluded on its own and the check it failed is
named, the run fails only if exclusions pass 10 percent, and the bound is checked before the
next paid call rather than after all 480. The fresh review asked for three more changes (decode
failures stay fatal with their original code, a stale failure code cleared after a good batch,
the early bound) and accepted the second round. Merged 18:23Z with `evals/social-classify-exclusion`.
Nothing about the social page is proven yet: the 13:40Z run today is the proving run, and the
page still says it has no data.

GitHub's scheduler drifted all afternoon. The hourly cron missed 16:17Z to 17:17Z and again
19:17Z to 21:17Z, after a gap earlier in the day; each time the supervisor dispatched it by
hand. At 21:25Z the tick gained a watchdog: when no hourly run is running and the newest one
started more than 90 minutes ago, it dispatches the workflow. Overnight it fired four times
(01:38Z, 03:09Z, 04:39Z, 06:10Z) against two scheduled fires, and every run succeeded. At 04:39Z
the watchdog and a late cron fired within two seconds of each other, so one hour got two runs;
harmless, and worth a tighter check later.

Byron answered the home-page judgement call by reply (issue #26): yes to the feed home, "in the
style of NBC's 2026 midterms hub". The intent (`intent/nz-election/home.md`) and a two-increment
plan (`plan-home.md`) were accepted by 09:30Z: a rule-separated feed with a countdown, seats,
polls, the six closest 2023 electorates on unchanged boundaries, social and method modules,
with today's polls page moving to `/polls/` and keeping every link fragment.

Then the supervisor stopped. From 00:08Z to 06:30Z today every tick that woke a run got "You've
reached your Fable limit": ten runs, six and a half hours, nothing reviewed or dispatched. The
first home build (H-a), planned for 00:05Z, went out at 06:42Z from a run on a different model.
The data jobs did not notice; the watchdog kept the hourly going without a supervisor.

## Decisions

- Feed home in the NBC hub style: Byron's yes on issue #26, acted on the same morning.
- Polls exception keyed to the row, not the revision; social exclusions per post with a 10
  percent bound instead of failing the whole run. Both reversible single changes, journalled.
- Hourly watchdog in `bin/tick`: a tooling change, journalled, not sent to Byron.

## What was learned

Both failures were the integration, not the code: a fixture revision standing in for a fact
about a row, and a response shape the tests never produced. Each had passed every test and two
reviews. The daily runs are the only test that matters for those, and they need someone reading
them; today that someone was a ten-minute tick, which is not an alert.

A scheduler you do not own needs a watchdog you do. The cron drift left three gaps of two to three hours each
before the supervisor noticed; the watchdog has held it since.

The supervisor is a single point of failure in a way Codex is not. The usage limit stopped all
review and dispatch for six hours. The data jobs carried on because they never depended on it.
That is the shape to keep for election night: nothing the live site needs should wait on a
supervisor run.
