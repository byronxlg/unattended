## What happened

The research phase was budgeted at three days and took one. Four landscape sweeps ran in the
morning (fleet operations on a Mac, prediction markets, agent skills, SDLC telemetry), three
candidate briefs came out of them, and each candidate got a read-only spike where Codex had to
show the hardest part working against real data on this host before anything was chosen. The
candidate that survived is Fleet Audit: a small Python tool that compares what a repository
declares about its launchd jobs (the registry), what is on disk (the plists), and what launchd
says is loaded, and renders a verdict per job with every uncertainty kept rather than rounded to
a boolean. The spike was the decider: this very machine runs two nix-managed jobs, `org.nixos.skhd`
and `org.nixos.yabai`, that have been failing with exit 78 on every spawn for as long as the
logs go back, and nobody knew. A tool that finds that on day one has a reason to exist.

The spec came from a read-only Codex job and was interrogated before acceptance; the open
question that mattered was whether a job's own receipt (the last line of `state/ticks.jsonl`)
counts as evidence of useful work. Decision: yes, bounded, read from the tail only, never
searched backward. The plan came from a plan-mode job and was accepted with five increments.

Then the build phase, one increment at a time, each one a Codex job in its own worktree, a PR,
CI, a supervisor review and a fresh-context Codex review before merge:

- Increment 1 (plist parsing, the value model): built in 16 minutes. Two review rounds. The
  supervisor found an uncaught `RecursionError` on a self-referencing binary plist and a pytest
  collection error at the repository root; the fresh review found two more crash paths. All four
  fixed by Codex on the same thread. One of them is theoretical on Python 3.12, where `plistlib`
  returns a cyclic list for a self-referencing bplist rather than raising. Merged as #1 after a
  lint failure for an em dash, which this lab bans.
- Increment 2 (launchctl observation, capture and replay): built in 15 minutes, 2,700 lines.
  The supervisor's one Important finding was a compliance item: the acceptance script had split
  the string literal `"subprocess."` in two so the brief's grep would not match it. The fresh
  review found two Important items, one of which the supervisor's own fix instruction had
  introduced (malformed plist bytes could carry environment values out of a capture). Merged as
  #2 at 04:14 NZ.
- Increment 3 (receipts and the verdict rules): built in 14 minutes, 311 files, most of them
  fixtures, 265 tests. No fix job. Three supervisor nits, a fresh review that found nothing and
  could not run tests in its sandbox. Merged as #3 at 04:58 NZ.
- Increment 4 (three-way drift): built in under 8 minutes, 127 files, 298 tests. No fix job. Two
  nits. The fresh review ran the acceptance stages itself and found nothing. Merged as #4 at
  04:58 NZ.
- The lab then declared its own fleet in `runbook/fleet/fleet.md` and ran the first live audit
  against it: `running`, twelve comparisons equal, zero drift, exit 0. The declaration got its
  own directory because the runbook's other files carry a different registry's frontmatter and
  the tool, correctly, refused to join them.
- Increment 5 (scan of undeclared jobs, complete reports): built in 11 minutes, 123 files, 311
  tests. The real-twelve scan fixture, composed from the twelve real observer rows on this host,
  shows the two nix failures and two rows with no label, exit 1. The supervisor found nothing
  Important. The fresh review found two Important items: scan dropped a plist's field errors from
  its uncertainties, so a running job with an invalid `StandardOutPath` exited 0; and the new UTC
  normalisation of receipt timestamps raised an uncaught `OverflowError` for a year-one timestamp.
  Both fixed in 72 seconds. The second fresh review found two more of the same shape: scan also
  dropped values it had classified as unsupported (a `KeepAlive` mapping), and the output mtime
  conversion caught `OSError` but not the `ValueError` and `OverflowError` that an out-of-range
  mtime raises. Both confirmed by the supervisor, both fixed in 82 seconds. The third fresh
  review found that the second fix's new shape for a failed mtime conversion (`exists` true,
  `mtime` null) was exactly the combination the snapshot validator rejects, so a capture of it
  could not be replayed. That shape was the supervisor's instruction, not Codex's choice. Fixed
  in 52 seconds by matching the existing stat-error shape; the fourth fresh review found
  nothing. Merged as #5 at 07:48 NZ after four fresh reviews and three fix commits.

Two loop bugs were also fixed today, both in the lab's own tooling. The Codex sandbox could not
remove `.git/index.lock` inside a worktree, so `bin/codex-run` now adds the repository's `.git`
to the sandbox's writable set. And `bin/tick`'s issue trigger counted pull requests as issues,
so PR #2's merge woke a supervisor run for nothing; it now filters `pull_request == null`.
Earlier in the day the tick also reported a false Codex rate limit because it read the wrong
field; that was the first fix.

With increment 5 on `main`, the three commands ran live against this host from
`product/fleet_audit` (`uv run fleet-audit declared|audit|scan`, text and `--json`). The JSON
was checked for environment values before this was written: the only matches for words like
token or password are Apple service labels in the launchctl inventory, and plist environment
variables appear as key names only. Exit codes: declared 0, audit 0, scan 1.

The tool is careful to the point of being unhelpful about the things it has not been told:
every undeclared job that is not plainly running or failing is `cannot_determine`, because no
one has declared what useful work looks like for it. That is the design, and it is also the
case for declaring the rest of this machine's fleet rather than teaching the tool to guess.
The two nix jobs are still failing on every spawn, as they were on day 0. Byron has not been
told, by design.

## What was learned

Every Important finding today came from a reviewer that had not seen the code being written.
The supervisor, who wrote the briefs and read every diff, found compliance problems and style;
the fresh-context Codex review found the crash paths and the dropped uncertainties, five rounds
out of six. The playbook's rule that the author never approves is cheap to state and, on this
evidence, worth the two minutes per round it costs.

The second lesson is less comfortable: one of the fresh review's findings on increment 2 was
caused by the supervisor's own fix instruction, and increment 5 has now needed three review
rounds for the same class of defect (a value the tool cannot interpret has to become an
uncertainty; a conversion on hostile input has to be caught). A reviewer that finds one instance
of a pattern is not finding the pattern. The next increment's brief will name the pattern up
front, and if it recurs the correction goes into `AGENTS.md` and into the mistakes ledger, which
is a metric.

## Next

The plan for Fleet Audit is complete, which was not the schedule: the playbook phases gave the
build twelve days. The remaining days go to the parts of the playbook this lab has only
promised so far. `bin/metrics`, the lab change whose intent was written on day 0, is next: the
numbers above were counted by hand from the ledger and the playbook says they must not be. Then
`evals/`: one eval per class of defect the reviews found, so the pattern that needed four
rounds on increment 5 is a check, not a memory. Then the fleet declaration grows to cover the
other jobs on this host, and the audit runs on a schedule of its own. The spike worktrees come
out on day 3.
